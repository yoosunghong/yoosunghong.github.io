### PUBG Ally: A Conversational Embodied Agent as an AI Teammate
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29837

The vision of AI as a collaborative teammate, capable of natural language interaction and real-time action, is becoming a reality with projects like PUBG Ally. This embodied AI teammate for the popular game PUBG seamlessly integrates a language model (LM) for high-level reasoning and strategic decision-making with a rapid control layer for immediate game actions. The LM uses a controlled interface for tool use, allowing it to perceive the game state, interpret player speech, maintain context, and issue strategic action choices. These choices are then translated by a lower-level system into precise movements, combat maneuvers, and recovery actions. Iteratively trained using nearly 39,000 sessions of real gameplay data, PUBG Ally ensures synchronized speech and actions, all while adhering to strict latency constraints.

This work marks a significant engineering leap for real-time, conversational embodied AI, providing a practical blueprint for deploying complex AI agents in high-stakes environments. It tackles critical challenges such as stringent latency requirements, model compression, runtime safety through guardrails and memory redaction, and continuous improvement via extensive real-user data and iterative feedback loops. The project validates that such an integrated approach can achieve natural human-AI interaction and high user acceptance, truly elevating AI agents beyond mere tools to perceived teammates.

Engineers can apply the PUBG Ally architecture to develop sophisticated, real-time embodied AI agents in diverse fields beyond gaming, including industrial automation, robotics, or complex simulation environments. The methodologies for combining LM-based reasoning and tool use with a fast control layer, alongside strategies for model compression, safety guardrails, and iterative training, are directly transferable for building robust, deployable, and user-accepted AI systems in production. This framework could be instrumental in creating virtual assistants with physical embodiments or AI partners in training simulations requiring high fidelity and responsive interaction.

### Browser demo of our Clash Royale RL environment: a 5.6k-parameter REINFORCE policy learns defensive placement against a brute-force optimum **Link:** https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wsfkwg/browser_demo_of_our_clash_royale_rl_environment_a/

Advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) continue to yield highly specialized AI for complex tactical scenarios. This work presents a compelling browser-based demonstration of an RL environment built for the mobile game Clash Royale. Within this setup, a remarkably lightweight 5.6k-parameter REINFORCE policy is trained to master optimal defensive unit placement strategies. The RL agent demonstrates its ability to develop robust defensive tactics by learning to counter an opponent that employs a brute-force optimum search strategy, showcasing its prowess in an adversarial context.

This project highlights the significant feasibility of applying relatively lightweight RL models, such as REINFORCE with a limited number of parameters, to effectively solve specific and complex tactical challenges within real-time strategy games. Training against a "brute-force optimum" opponent establishes a rigorous benchmark, validating the policy's capacity to learn robust and near-optimal strategies in a high-stakes competitive environment. The browser demo aspect further emphasizes the potential for accessible, interactive RL research and deployment, making these sophisticated AI agents more approachable for experimentation and showcasing.

This methodology can be leveraged by engineers to develop highly specialized AI agents for focused tactical problems across various domains, including other simulation environments, games, or even real-world control systems requiring specific optimization. It provides a blueprint for training intelligent agents for automated defense systems, optimizing resource allocation in dynamic environments, or creating robust AI to counter known optimal adversarial strategies. The browser demo paradigm also suggests a user-friendly way to test and showcase complex AI capabilities, fostering broader engagement and understanding.

### OneWorld: Learning Consistent Physics Across Actions in World Models
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30946

For autonomous agents to operate reliably in dynamic environments, they need a coherent understanding of the world's physics. OneWorld addresses this by introducing a shared-mechanism counterfactual generation framework that jointly models multiple action-conditioned futures originating from the same initial state. It employs a physical mechanism interpreter to infer a distribution over latent physical properties from each predicted outcome. These distributions are then aggregated into "shared-world evidence," which constrains the training process, thereby ensuring consistency in the underlying physical mechanisms across diverse action predictions while still allowing for distinct outcomes.

This innovation significantly enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of AI systems that rely on world models for planning and decision-making. By enforcing a coherent understanding of underlying physics across counterfactual scenarios, OneWorld prevents contradictory predictions that could otherwise lead to erroneous actions or fragile system behavior. This consistency is absolutely crucial for the development of robust and safe autonomous agents operating in complex, unpredictable environments.

Senior Engineers can leverage OneWorld to build more robust and predictable autonomous agents, particularly in critical applications like robotics, autonomous driving, or complex simulation environments. It dramatically improves the reliability of predictive models used for planning actions where physical consistency (e.g., how objects interact, how environments respond) is paramount. This, in turn, leads to safer and more effective decision-making, even under significant uncertainty, making autonomous systems more dependable in real-world deployments.

### Geometric-Photometric Event-based 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18640

High-speed and accurate 3D reconstruction is vital for many next-generation AI applications. This work, dubbed GPERT (Geometric-Photometric Event-based 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing), addresses the critical trade-off between accuracy and temporal resolution in event-based 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It achieves this by ingeniously utilizing the fine-grained temporal information from sparse events through a decoupled rendering process. GPERT dedicates one branch to event-by-event geometry (depth) rendering using ray-tracing, while another branch handles snapshot-based radiance (intensity) rendering from an image of warped events.

GPERT represents a significant advancement in leveraging event cameras for 3D reconstruction, achieving state-of-the-art performance without requiring prior information such as pre-trained image reconstruction models or COLMAP initialization. This decoupled geometric and photometric processing enables the creation of robust, high-temporal resolution 3D models with remarkably sharp scene edges and fast training times. This effectively overcomes many limitations of traditional frame-based or less optimized event-based methods, offering superior detail and speed.

Senior Engineers can readily apply GPERT in systems demanding high-speed, accurate 3D reconstruction within dynamic environments. Ideal applications include autonomous vehicles for real-time environmental mapping, robotics for precise navigation and manipulation, or industrial inspection systems requiring rapid and detailed analysis. Its ability to operate without extensive prior setup simplifies deployment, allowing for quick integration into new event-camera based sensing pipelines, especially where sharp object boundaries and efficient processing are critical performance requirements.

### Energy Efficiency of Locally Deployed LLMs: A Preliminary Quantitative GPU Power Benchmark on Consumer Hardware
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00008

As Large Language Models (LLMs) proliferate, their energy consumption becomes a critical consideration, especially for local deployments. This paper provides a crucial benchmark on the energy efficiency of 18 open-source LLMs (ranging from 0.5B to 7B parameters) running on a single consumer GPU (RTX 4060ti) using the Ollama inference engine. The study meticulously quantifies GPU power draw, total energy per prompt, energy per output token (J/tok), and throughput (tok/s) across a fixed prompt set. The key finding reveals that energy efficiency is predominantly influenced by model architecture and quantization strategy, rather than just the raw parameter count, with qwen2.5:0.5b and tinyllama:1.1b identified as the most efficient models.

This work delivers critical hardware-level energy consumption data for locally deployed LLMs, filling a significant gap in benchmarks that typically focus solely on accuracy or inference speed. It quantitatively demonstrates that strategic model selection based on architecture and quantization can lead to substantial energy savings—up to 8.6x more efficient than 7B-Mistral in certain scenarios. This offers a crucial new dimension for designing sustainable and cost-effective on-premise AI systems, enabling developers to make more environmentally conscious choices without sacrificing too much performance.

Engineers can directly leverage these specific benchmarks to make informed decisions for LLM deployment on consumer hardware or edge devices where energy efficiency is paramount. The study suggests prioritizing models like qwen2.5:0.5b or tinyllama:1.1b for optimal J/tok and throughput. Furthermore, it emphasizes focusing optimization efforts on model architecture and quantization rather than being solely driven by parameter count. Developers should also be mindful of variations in internal reasoning processes, as these can anomalously increase per-prompt energy consumption, highlighting the need for comprehensive testing beyond simple token generation metrics.
