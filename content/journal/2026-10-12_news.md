### Total Annihilation, rebuilt for modern computers
**Link:** https://nanolathe.gg

**Core Content:** Nanolathe is an open-source, MIT-licensed 2.5D RTS engine that meticulously re-implements the classic Total Annihilation through clean-room research. It features a modern GPU renderer that applies advanced lighting, glow, and richer detail to the original game assets, while also offering a classic rendering mode. The engine is cross-platform, running natively on Windows, Mac, and Linux, with a browser-based demo available.

**Technical Significance:** This project showcases a robust example of reverse-engineering and modern re-implementation of a complex legacy game engine. It highlights successful integration of a modern rendering pipeline into an existing asset structure and demonstrates strategies for achieving cross-platform compatibility and visual enhancements for older software without altering core game logic.

**Practical Application:** Engineers can leverage Nanolathe's MIT-licensed codebase and documented formats as a robust foundation for developing new 2.5D real-time strategy games or exploring advanced rendering techniques for legacy content. It serves as a practical blueprint for modernizing other classic game titles, porting legacy applications to contemporary platforms, or as an educational tool for understanding game architecture and graphics programming.

### How I reverse engineered a commercial spatial audio effect
**Link:** http://epestr.com/blog/reverse-engineering-a-commercial-spatial-audio-effect/

**Core Content:** The author reverse-engineered a commercial spatial audio effect by treating it as a black box and empirically analyzing its input-output behavior. The core technical mechanism involved feeding an impulse (a single non-zero sample) into the commercial software and recording the resulting output, which represents the filter's impulse response. Assuming the effect is a Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) system, this impulse response fully characterizes its audio processing behavior, revealing aspects like decay, shape, and phase delays.

**Technical Significance:** This methodology offers a powerful, model-agnostic approach to analyze and replicate complex audio processing algorithms without direct access to their internal implementation details. It underscores the utility of fundamental signal processing concepts, like impulse response measurement and LTI system theory, as practical tools for understanding and emulating sophisticated proprietary effects. This technique is invaluable when faced with unknown or closed-source audio systems, enabling engineers to deduce their functional characteristics from observable behavior.

**Practical Application:** Engineers can utilize this impulse response capture method to recreate specific spatial audio effects from existing hardware or software within new projects, especially for platforms where commercial alternatives might be lacking. This allows for the development of custom DSP plugins, virtual reality audio engines, or game audio systems that precisely emulate desired sound characteristics. It's also applicable for quality control, benchmarking new implementations against a reference, or even for educational purposes to deconstruct complex audio processes.

### I trained a 414k-parameter transformer to fly a boids flock, then tested whether the rules a probe can read are the ones it uses **Link:** https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x39jlu/i_trained_a_414kparameter_transformer_to_fly_a/

**Core Content:** This project trains a 414k-parameter transformer to emulate the emergent flocking behavior of a boids simulation. The primary investigation explores whether the explicit underlying rules of boids (separation, alignment, cohesion) are directly readable via interpretability probes from the transformer's internal states. This setup aims to uncover if a neural network, when tasked with replicating a system driven by simple rules, learns to internalize those specific rules or develops its own, potentially different, internal mechanisms to achieve the same external behavior.

**Technical Significance:** For senior engineers, this work underscores the critical challenges in AI interpretability and model understanding, particularly for systems exhibiting emergent complexity. It questions the assumption that trained models necessarily internalize domain-specific rules in an easily extractable form, highlighting a potential disconnect between observed behavior and interpretable internal representations. This has profound implications for model validation, debugging, and establishing trust in autonomous or critical AI systems where understanding "why" a decision is made is paramount.

**Practical Application:** This methodology can be applied to engineering AI agents for complex adaptive systems, such as optimizing traffic flow, managing swarm robotics, or simulating economic models, where the underlying "rules" are critical to understand. Engineers can use similar probing techniques to debug unexpected behaviors in learned control policies, verify adherence to design principles (even if implicitly learned), or potentially extract high-level "rules" from complex models to simplify them for deployment in resource-constrained or explainability-mandated environments.
